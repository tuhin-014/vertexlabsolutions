SuppSage — Medical Disclaimer & How SageScore Works

Last updated: July 17, 2026

SuppSage provides educational information about dietary supplements. It is not medical advice, and it is not a substitute for a licensed healthcare professional. Always talk to your doctor or pharmacist before starting, stopping, or changing any supplement or medication — especially if you are pregnant, nursing, have a medical condition, or take prescription drugs.

Not evaluated by the FDA

Dietary supplements are not reviewed or approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for safety or effectiveness before they are sold. Statements in SuppSage have not been evaluated by the FDA, and nothing in the app is intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

We do not test products

SuppSage does not perform laboratory testing of supplements. The SageScore is calculated from public, authoritative data sources, including:

  • The NIH Dietary Supplement Label Database (DSLD) — product labels and ingredients;
  • The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements and the Dietary Reference Intakes (DRI) — recommended amounts and safe upper limits;
  • FDA enforcement reports — product recalls;
  • Published third-party certification listings (USP, NSF, Informed Sport); and
  • Curated, citation-backed reference tables for ingredient forms, effective dose ranges, and interactions.

A high SageScore reflects favorable public data (clear labeling, well-absorbed forms, evidence-informed doses, no recalls or flagged ingredients). It is not a guarantee of quality, purity, safety, or results, and it is not a laboratory certificate.

Label data may contain errors

Product information is transcribed from manufacturer labels and may be incomplete, outdated, or incorrect. Reformulations may not be reflected immediately. Always verify against the physical product label and the manufacturer.

Interactions & recall alerts are advisory

Interaction warnings and recall alerts are general, advisory signals based on public data and fuzzy matching (for example, by brand name). They may be incomplete or may not apply to your exact product. They are not a substitute for professional advice. If you believe you have a recalled product, stop using it and consult FDA.gov and your healthcare provider.

"Effects" tracking is correlation, not proof

If you log how you feel over time, any patterns SuppSage surfaces are associations, not proof of cause and effect. Many factors affect how you feel — sleep, stress, diet, activity, and more. A change that appears alongside a supplement does not mean the supplement caused it. Use these insights as a starting point for a conversation with your healthcare provider, not as medical conclusions.

Individual needs vary

Recommended amounts and safe limits differ by age, sex, pregnancy status, health conditions, and medications. SuppSage uses general reference values and, optionally, the age and sex you provide. It cannot account for your full medical situation.
