Where do I find the will, and what do I do with it?

Part of Settle's executor guides · 31 jurisdictions · state content checked 2026-09 · page generated 2026-09-27

Check the safe, the filing cabinet, the safe-deposit box and the office of any attorney they used. Whoever holds the original usually has a legal duty to deliver it to the court within a set window after learning of the death — how long depends on the state.

What this involves

The original signed will is required to open probate.

How: Check the safe, files, and safe-deposit box; ask their attorney.

Applies in 25 of the 31 jurisdictions Settle covers.

Where it works differently

6 jurisdictions handle this their own way:

California

California requires whoever holds the will to deliver it to the superior court clerk within 30 days of learning of the death, and to mail a copy to the executor named in it (Prob. Code §8200).

How: Check the safe, files, and safe-deposit box; ask their attorney. Deliver the original to the county superior court clerk.

Georgia

The original signed will is required to petition for probate.

New York

The original signed will is required to file for probate in Surrogate's Court.

North Carolina

The original signed will is required to apply for probate with the Clerk of Superior Court.

Pennsylvania

The original signed will is required to obtain letters from the Register of Wills.

Texas

In Texas a will must be filed for probate within 4 years of death, or the estate is generally treated as if there were no will. The original is required.
